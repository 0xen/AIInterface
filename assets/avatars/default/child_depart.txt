# child_depart: a worker has been sent off. The slime *splits in two* -- the
# body goes wide, a second head rises out of the left flank, the waist pinches
# to a neck and lets go, and for a beat there are plainly two of them standing
# side by side. The second one is a third the parent's size, and it leaves on
# its own feet: three strides to the left and out past the edge. The parent is
# left whole. 14 fps, loop=false, 1.36 s.
#
# The *child* leaves here, not the avatar. M2.3c's `depart` is the whole slime
# walking out of the band, and it ends on an empty frame; this one ends on
# idle, because the parent is still standing there when it is over. They are
# separate triggers for exactly that reason -- `child_depart` and `child_merge`
# are the worker pair, `depart` is the avatar's own exit.
#
# **Why a split and not a bud.** The first drawing of this clip budded a blob
# off the shoulder and let it drift out of the top-left corner. A bud reads as
# the body shedding something; what has happened is that a *worker* has gone,
# which is a second creature, and a second creature has to be seen standing on
# its own before it can be believed to have walked away. So the whole
# silhouette divides, and the beat at frame four -- two bodies, two faces, a
# column of daylight between them -- is the frame the clip exists to reach.
#
# **How "smaller" is drawn.** The child is 6x5 against the parent's 16x16, and
# it wears one-cell eyes where the parent wears two-by-two blocks. At 16 px a
# cell that difference in the eye is what carries the size, more than the
# outline does: the same silhouette with the parent's face on it would read as
# the avatar standing further away.
#
# **How "walks" is drawn.** Two poses, alternating: a stride (taller, feet
# apart at the corners) and a passing position (squashed a cell, feet together
# under the middle). That is a gait, and it is the one thing a drift cannot
# fake -- the bottom row changes shape between frames, so the body is pushing
# off something instead of being carried.
#
# There is no face on the frame where the body is dividing, nor on the one
# where the child is down to two columns at the edge. A shape in the middle of
# becoming something has no eyes in it, and eyes at the very edge read as a
# drawing being dragged; wake_slide withholds them on its fast frames for the
# same reason.
#
# It leaves by the *left* on purpose, and along the floor. The right of the
# grid is where every accessory hangs (the thought bubble, the zZz, the mute
# cross), so a child going that way would walk into whichever one comes back
# up when the clip ends; and wake_slide already made the left the road the
# slime arrives by. Things come and go from the left here. The floor is what
# is new: the bud drifted out of the upper-left corner because it had no feet,
# and a thing with feet has to leave along the ground it is standing on.
#
# The parent narrows and leans right while the child is being made, and
# spreads back to full width behind it as it goes. That is what buys the room:
# sixteen columns will not hold a full-width parent and a child at once, so
# the parent gives up the left half for four frames and takes it back. It also
# happens to be what a body that has just let go of a third of itself does.
#
# The child leaves the 16x16 grid, not the band: a cell outside the grid is
# simply not drawn, which is what makes leaving free. With no accessory up the
# body is centred in a 22-cell stage, so the child winks out about three cells
# short of the band's own edge -- at 16 px a cell that is a small slime
# stepping off the corner, which is the read that matters. Reaching the band's
# true edge would need a placement, and a placement enlarges the envelope and
# costs scale off the body in every frame of every clip, showing or not.
#
# The last frame is idle.txt's first frame, cell for cell. Same contract the
# two entrances keep: a one-shot holds its last drawing until the policy asks
# for another clip, so ending anywhere else would leave the slime resting in a
# pose the breathe does not start from.
