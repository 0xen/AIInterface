# teaching: the slime explaining something to you, in a mortarboard. 8 fps.
#
# The cap is drawn in the @overlay layer of these frames, not as a sprite. A
# sprite pinned above the head would enlarge the stage envelope and scale the
# whole avatar down to fit -- one cell of headroom costs sixteen pixels off the
# body -- whereas the overlay is the character's own 16x16 grid and costs
# nothing. It also happens to be the truthful drawing: a worn hat overlaps the
# crown, it does not hover over it. The board overhangs the dome on both sides
# because that is the one feature that makes a mortarboard a mortarboard, and
# the tassel hangs clear of the silhouette on the right so it stays legible.
#
# What makes it read as *explaining* rather than as a slime in a hat is the
# rhythm: three quick beats, each one a lean and an open mouth, then hold=7 --
# most of a second of stillness with the mouth shut, which is the pause after
# a point where you look at the person to see if they got it. The cap moves
# with the body on every frame, so it is worn rather than pasted on.
