# child_depart_quick: the other way a worker gets sent off (the second variant
# behind the `child_depart` trigger -- not M2.3c's `depart`, which is the
# avatar's own exit). Same event as child_depart.txt and the same three things
# have to be true of it: the body divides, the second one is smaller, and it
# leaves on its feet. What differs is where the division happens and how long
# it takes. 16 fps, loop=false, 0.81 s against child_depart's 1.36.
#
# This one splits from the *bottom*. The parent squashes flat, shuts its eyes
# and squeezes, a lump extrudes out of the base on the left while the body
# snaps up tall and thin above it, and the lump lets go already on the floor
# and already walking. child_depart.txt divides from the top down and watches
# it happen; this is a body getting something out of itself and going straight
# back to what it was doing.
#
# **This replaced a fling.** The variant here used to throw the child off the
# top edge in two frames, which was a fine second reading of a bud drifting
# away and is the wrong second reading of a split: a thrown child does not
# walk, and a variation that contradicts the thing it is a variation of is not
# a variation, it is a second animation that happens to share a trigger. The
# two have to differ in *shape* -- that is the lesson wake_slide was drawn to
# -- so they differ in which end the body divides at, and in how hard it is
# working, and not in whether the child has feet.
#
# The trigger keeps two drawings for the reason it always had: spawn three
# tasks in a session and one departure played three times reads as a loop, and
# avatar_children_test asserts the count rather than the names.
#
# No face on the squeeze or on the stub at the edge, for the reason every
# fast clip here withholds one.
#
# Ends on idle.txt's first frame, as every one of the child's clips does --
# the parent is still standing in the band when this is over.
